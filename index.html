JOURNAL OF CLINICAL NURSING

Living with a stoma: self-management needs and educational priorities

P. Andersson, M. Clarke, R. Patel

Abstract

Individuals adapting to a newly formed stoma require practical knowledge, confidence and access to timely specialist support. This qualitative study explored the educational priorities described by adults during the first six months following discharge.

Results

Participants described a transition from dependence to self-management. Confidence increased when education was repeated, practical and tailored to the person's changing needs. Participants particularly valued opportunities to rehearse appliance changes and discuss complications before discharge.

Uncertainty was most pronounced when written information differed from advice received verbally. Follow-up contact with a stoma care nurse was described as an important source of reassurance and problem solving.

My note

This could support the argument that education should be staged rather than delivered only once before discharge.
